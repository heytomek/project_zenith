# Zenith reference research

Desk research · October 4, 2026. Scope corrected by the owner after the initial operator-focused recommendation. Existing products covering a function is acceptable; competitive overlap does not limit Zenith's ambition. The current direction is [strategic coordination for humans and agents](product_vision.md), with philosophy, shared primitives and a high-level human decision experience.

Sources below are official product documentation and public-sector reporting. They establish documented capabilities and problem context, not independently measured product quality. No customers were interviewed and willingness to pay has not been established. The earlier maintenance-first buyer hypothesis is superseded; it does not govern the roadmap.

## What the research is for

Use incumbents to identify useful capabilities, integration opportunities and standards Zenith should meet or exceed. Zenith can compete on the coherence of its decision model, strategic understanding, human/agent coordination, epistemic transparency, execution and learning. These are intended advantages to demonstrate, not measured superiority.

A market gap is one possible reason to build. A substantially better implementation of an existing category is another. Neither approach removes the need to establish useful decisions and compare results against alternatives. The product's large scope comes from the owner's endstate ambition and recursive coordination model, not from an uncovered feature in a vendor checklist.

Foundry's guide emphasizes a named decision, simulated alternatives, action and shared state. That is a relevant functional standard. Zenith should go further in its intended strategic scope: explicit ends and protections, dependencies across programs, bounded agent delegation, contested evidence and outcome-driven revision. Those differences describe the proposed design, not a finding that Foundry cannot support them. [Operational applications guide](https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/app-building/operational-apps)

## Mission-adjacent infrastructure

[Open Referral](https://openreferral.org/about/technology-overview/) supplies interoperable human-service directory formats. [Unite Us](https://uniteus.com/products/closed-loop-referral-system/) documents cross-provider referrals, service-specific outcomes and integrations. These are potential source or execution interfaces as well as adjacent products.

Zenith can coordinate interventions informed by such systems. A listed service is not necessarily available capacity, and a completed referral is not necessarily relief of unmet need. Capabilities require conditions and existing commitments; outcomes require attributable evidence. A strategic model must preserve these distinctions as it aggregates across sources.

## Domain context retained from the earlier investigation

England's Regulator of Social Housing reports £10.0 billion of private registered providers' repairs and maintenance spending for the year ending March 2025, including £3.9 billion capitalised. Financial capacity remained constrained. The accounts cover 200 large provider groups and exclude council-owned housing finances. This is evidence of consequential resource decisions, not a £10 billion software market or a reason to define Zenith around housing operators. [2025 Global Accounts, published January 15, 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/2025-global-accounts-of-private-registered-providers/2025-global-accounts-of-private-registered-providers)

The Housing Ombudsman's 2025–26 review identifies repairs as the largest source of severe failings, including delays, poor records and inadequate risk assessment. Complaints are a selected population, not a representative prevalence estimate. Allocation or coordination software cannot be assumed to remedy those failures. [Annual Complaints Review 2025–26](https://www.housing-ombudsman.org.uk/annual-complaint-review-reports/annual-complaints-review-2025-26/)

Housing may be one component of a strategic energy–housing–workforce demonstration. It does not establish the intended user's level, product boundary or full moral scope.

## Implications for the next demonstration

- Demonstrate one consequential strategy spanning several domains and nested or overlapping scopes.
- Connect heterogeneous source claims to actual model inputs; preserve uncertainty and contradictions.
- Compare coherent intervention portfolios under explicit objectives, policy and shared-resource constraints.
- Translate a human choice into acknowledged, bounded agent mandates and attributable outcomes.
- Show how a changed assumption or failed dependency creates a new strategic decision.
- Exercise the same contracts in a smaller scope without introducing a different product ontology.

Decision usefulness, policy enforcement, source traceability, coordination correctness and human comprehension are testable now. Future superiority, commercial adoption and civilization-scale benefit remain things to establish through implementation and evidence. Incumbent overlap is not a rejection criterion. [The roadmap](roadmap.md) and [validation protocol](usefulness-test.md) define the next tests.
